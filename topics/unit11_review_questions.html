LIU08106 / RIU08106 · Unit 11

Review Questions

Legal, Ethical and Inclusive Multimedia Practice

← Back to Unit 11 on the course site

Answer using explanations and justified examples. Refer to official source documents for legal requirements; avoid making unsupported general claims.

Copyright and permission

  1. Define copyright and explain what kind of multimedia works may receive protection.
  2. Distinguish ownership of a physical photograph from copyright ownership.
  3. Why does scanning not automatically grant online publication rights?
  4. Give four fields that should appear in a rights register.
  5. Why is “I found it online” not an adequate reuse permission?
  6. Explain why educational use requires careful legal assessment rather than blanket assumptions.

Creative Commons

  1. Explain BY, SA, NC and ND in a licence.
  2. Compare CC BY and CC BY-ND for distributing an adapted illustration.
  3. Distinguish CC0 from a CC BY licence.
  4. Write a complete fictional photograph attribution with a licence link.
  5. Why might a commercial exhibition conflict with CC BY-NC?
  6. Can a CC licence settle the privacy rights of people in a photograph? Explain.

Privacy, consent and data protection

  1. Give four examples of personal data in multimedia archives.
  2. Differentiate consent to recording and consent to unrestricted online distribution.
  3. Explain why consent is not the only possible lawful basis for processing personal data.
  4. Distinguish a record retention obligation from an online disclosure decision.
  5. What additional safeguards may apply when children appear in media?
  6. Explain data minimisation with an oral-history example.

Inclusive media and accessibility

  1. Distinguish captions, subtitles, transcript and audio description.
  2. What is the WCAG AA contrast requirement for normal text and large text?
  3. Design a meaningful alt-text alternative for a photograph used to convey a historical fact.
  4. Describe a keyboard-only test of an interactive library tour.
  5. Why should colour not be the only indicator of a required action?
  6. List evidence that should be recorded after an accessibility test.

AI, authenticity and ethics

  1. Explain two risks of AI-generated catalogue descriptions.
  2. Why should a synthetic historical reconstruction be disclosed?
  3. What precautions apply before uploading an interview to an external AI tool?
  4. Give an example where legality and community expectations might diverge.
  5. Describe a process for handling a publication complaint.
  6. Distinguish an original archival object from an edited public derivative.

Applied BLISS/BRIM scenarios

  1. Plan the legal and ethical checks for a BLISS online photograph exhibition.
  2. Evaluate a BRIM record containing a third party’s private address.
  3. Show how to check that redacted text is truly absent from exported files.
  4. Prepare a five-item rights/access register for a fictional collection.
  5. Design a public-facing media credit with a link to a Creative Commons licence.
  6. Explain how the Unit 11 decisions should support the Unit 12 multimedia capstone.
