LIU08106 / RIU08106 · Unit 03

Review Questions and Applied Exercises

Evaluate capture, formats, editing, source integrity and OCR through practical cases.

Part A — Concepts and capture

  1. Distinguish image capture, image processing and multimedia image delivery, with one example from an information service.
  2. Compare camera capture, flatbed scanning and sheet-fed scanning. Give one suitability concern for each.
  3. Why does scanning a fragile bound archival register require different handling from scanning loose modern office copies?
  4. Define image focus, glare, skew and clipping. Explain how each can affect reading.
  5. Explain how a scanning resolution choice relates to physical dimensions and smallest meaningful detail.
  6. Why is “always scan at 600 PPI” a poor universal rule?

Part B — Formats, copies and edits

  1. Compare TIFF, PNG, JPEG and SVG based on their typical purposes and limitations.
  2. Explain why a TIFF file may be compressed and why a JPEG file does not prove an image is authentic.
  3. Distinguish a managed master, a working copy, an access derivative and a thumbnail.
  4. Explain why a cropped archival access image must be linked to the full original/master.
  5. Distinguish cropping from resizing, and explain why upsampling cannot invent authentic fine detail.
  6. Explain how brightness, contrast, sharpening and denoising can affect evidence.
  7. Design a short image-edit log with fields that could be useful for both librarians and archivists.
  8. Give five quality checks for a freshly captured document image.

Part C — Calculations and workflow

  1. A 5-inch-wide item is scanned at 400 PPI. Estimate the pixel width. Show the formula.
  2. A 2400 × 1600 image is downsampled to 1200 pixels wide with the aspect ratio preserved. Calculate the new height.
  3. Calculate the approximate uncompressed sample bytes and MiB of a 1200 × 800, 24-bit RGB image.
  4. Explain why a 1200 × 800 JPEG saved at one quality setting can be smaller than the same image stored as PNG.
  5. Write an eight-stage workflow for producing a website access copy without altering the controlled master.
  6. Develop a quality-control checklist for a two-page letter where faint pencil marks must be retained.

Part D — OCR and accessibility

  1. Define OCR. What is the difference between an image-only scan and an image with verified searchable text?
  2. The OCR text is “L1BRARY REC0RDS 1984”. Identify the likely errors and explain why they matter.
  3. Describe three factors that can cause OCR errors and three ways to reduce or detect them.
  4. Write useful alternative text and an accompanying description for an image of a handwritten historical letter.
  5. Explain what changes when processing Kiswahili or bilingual materials in an OCR workflow.

Part E — Professional applications

  1. BLISS: Plan a web-accessible image gallery for a university library orientation. Identify outputs, image sizes, captions, rights and accessibility.
  2. BRIM: Plan the capture of a bound historical register. State preservation concerns and master/derivative controls.
  3. BLISS: A book-cover image becomes blurry on a large poster. Explain what occurred and propose a suitable solution.
  4. BRIM: A cropped exhibition photograph hides a handwritten annotation. Explain the risk and how to remedy it.
  5. Prepare a one-page decision note comparing two image formats for a mixed collection of photographs and textual records.

Self-check prompts

How to answer technical questions

Define the term; identify the source, audience and requirement; explain the trade-off; then provide an example. A file extension alone is not enough evidence of preservation suitability.

Helpful calculations

Pixels = inches × PPI. Preserved-height resize = old height × new width / old width. Uncompressed RGB payload in bytes = width × height × 3 for 24-bit RGB. MiB = bytes / 1,048,576.

Professional responsibility

State which file is the master and which is a derivative; preserve contextual information; record relevant edits; verify OCR; consider rights and accessibility.
