Unit 12

Review Questions

Publishing, evaluation and integration across the multimedia course.

Project definition and users

  1. Distinguish a multimedia deliverable from a usable multimedia information service.
  2. Write a problem statement for a BLISS digital library guide.
  3. Write a problem statement for a BRIM archive discovery portal.
  4. Identify five audience characteristics that affect media delivery.
  5. Give three measurable success criteria for a small multimedia prototype.
  6. Explain how a minimum viable scope protects project quality.

Media production and architecture

  1. Identify eight fields in a media inventory.
  2. Explain the source–derivative relationship with an audio example.
  3. Why must a storyboard follow a realistic user task?
  4. Outline a simple five-page information architecture.
  5. Explain why case-sensitive file names matter on web hosting.
  6. Describe two low-bandwidth adaptations for a multimedia service.

Metadata, ethics and access

  1. Identify six useful fields in a digital object catalogue record.
  2. Explain why a stable identifier matters after a file rename.
  3. Distinguish an archival description from access to a restricted source item.
  4. What evidence supports the legal reuse of a photograph?
  5. Why must AI-generated descriptive claims be verified?
  6. Give two privacy risks in online search snippets.

Accessibility and quality

  1. Explain alt text, captions, transcript and audio description.
  2. Describe a keyboard-only test of a multimedia portal.
  3. Why cannot an automatic accessibility checker certify an entire website?
  4. Distinguish quality assurance from quality control.
  5. List six publication-release checks and one piece of evidence for each.
  6. Identify two critical blockers and two minor release issues.

Usability, measurement and improvement

  1. Define a realistic task-based usability test and its participant instructions.
  2. Calculate task completion for 9 successful attempts out of 12 attempts.
  3. Find the median of task times 24, 34, 30, 80 and 36 seconds.
  4. Why is observed success among three classmates not a population estimate?
  5. Describe a useful defect report and retest evidence.
  6. Explain when a privacy defect should override an impact–effort matrix.

Capstone and professional presentation

  1. List the mandatory components of a BLISS three-item exhibition.
  2. List the mandatory components of a BRIM fictional records portal.
  3. Outline a fair 100-mark instructional project rubric.
  4. What should a final demonstration show instead of only listing features?
  5. Explain how to document rights, access and version changes after publication.
  6. Summarise how Units 1–11 contribute to the applied Unit 12 project.
