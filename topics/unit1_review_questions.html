LIU08106 / RIU08106 · Unit 01

Review Questions & Applied Exercises

Use these questions to explain ideas in your own words and connect them to professional practice.

Part A — Understand the concepts

  1. Define multimedia and explain why the use of a computer alone does not automatically make a resource multimedia.
  2. Describe six multimedia elements, giving one information-service example for each.
  3. Explain how text and images can complement each other in a digital collection.
  4. Distinguish multimedia from hypertext and hypermedia, with a real example of each.
  5. Differentiate linear, non-linear and hybrid multimedia presentations. When would you choose each?
  6. Explain analogue and digital media, giving two examples of each.
  7. Describe digitisation and explain why scanning a document is only one step in a complete workflow.
  8. Identify four components of a multimedia system and describe their roles.

Part B — Interpret and apply

  1. A library website has headings, a photograph of the building, a video tutorial and clickable subject categories. Identify the multimedia elements and explain which feature creates hypermedia navigation.
  2. An archives exhibition displays scanned photographs alongside oral-history audio. Explain what additional context should accompany the media to support reliability and appropriate reuse.
  3. Why is a fixed-sequence documentary a multimedia resource even if viewers cannot choose different paths?
  4. Identify three benefits and three challenges of multimedia in a university information service. Propose a response to each challenge.
  5. Suggest four ways to improve accessibility in a video-based information service.
  6. A student has a limited mobile-data budget. Compare a 300 MB autoplay video with a text-first guide and optional video. Which design would you recommend and why?
  7. Explain how digital access copies can reduce the handling of fragile originals. Why must preservation of the source still be considered?

Part C — Programme-specific scenarios

BLISS scenario

16. A library plans to introduce an interactive orientation resource for new students. Propose a target audience, a realistic user problem, three multimedia elements with explanations, a navigation structure and one success measure.

17. A digital library adds book-cover images to a catalogue. Evaluate when this is useful and identify one accessibility and one rights-management consideration.

BRIM scenario

18. A district archive plans an online exhibition with scanned files, photographs and recorded recollections. Explain how multimedia enhances access and identify three safeguards to protect historical context, permission or authenticity.

19. An employee wants to upload a restricted administrative record to a public video presentation. Identify the risks and outline the steps that should occur before any publication.

Part D — Short design task

20. Draw a simple navigation sketch for a three-section multimedia resource. Label the linear or non-linear paths and show where text, images and audio or video would appear. In approximately 200 words, justify the choices you have made.

How to prepare strong answers. Begin with a clear definition. Explain how the concept works. Use one specific example from a library, records office or archive. Where the question asks you to evaluate or justify a choice, identify benefits and limitations rather than giving a list of terms.

Brief self-check pointers

Attempt the questions before using these prompts. They indicate the expected reasoning rather than replacing your full answer.

Questions 1–4: key distinctions

Multimedia combines media with a purpose; hypertext links text; hypermedia enables linked movement across media. Interaction is possible but not essential for multimedia.

Questions 5–8: structures and systems

Linear means a guided order; non-linear allows selection; hybrid mixes them. Analogue uses continuous physical variation; digital uses discrete encoded data. Capture, editing, storage, delivery and people form a system.

Questions 9–15: application and evaluation

Identify the media first, then state what each contributes. Consider user purpose, access, captions/transcripts, bandwidth, rights, accuracy, documentation and preservation of originals.

Questions 16–20: professional scenarios

BLISS scenarios benefit from service discovery and learning outcomes. BRIM scenarios also require provenance, authenticity, restriction review and chain of documentation. Explain the user path and justify each chosen medium.

These questions are for study and formative practice. They are not a published final examination.
