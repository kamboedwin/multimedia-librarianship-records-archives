Unit 10

Review Questions

Digital Preservation, Security and Continuity

← Back to Unit 10 on the course site

Use the notes, practical files and your own explanation. Calculations should show assumptions and units.

Understanding preservation

  1. Define digital preservation and explain why simply storing a file is insufficient.
  2. Distinguish integrity, authenticity, availability and confidentiality using one media object.
  3. Explain why descriptive metadata and provenance are preservation requirements.
  4. Give three physical and three logical threats to digital holdings.
  5. Distinguish a preservation master from an access derivative.
  6. Describe a lifecycle from selection and ingest to monitoring and access.

Integrity and fixity

  1. Define a file checksum and identify what SHA-256 represents.
  2. Explain why a checksum match is not evidence that a file originated from a genuine source.
  3. List the essential fields in a fixity manifest.
  4. What should be done when a trusted baseline and later digest do not match?
  5. Why must an ingest baseline be supported by additional validation?
  6. Explain why a migrated file normally has a different checksum.

Storage and continuity

  1. Distinguish backups from replication and archival storage.
  2. Describe the 3-2-1 heuristic and give one important limitation.
  3. Propose three reasonably independent copies for a small library collection.
  4. Explain why an encrypted backup needs a documented key recovery method.
  5. Define RPO, with a four-hourly backup example.
  6. Define RTO and list the operations counted within recovery.
  7. A 6-hour backup cycle is used. Can it meet a 90-minute RPO? Explain.
  8. A restoration test takes 5 hours. Evaluate a target RTO of 2 hours.
  9. Design a short test-restoration checklist.

Security and governance

  1. Describe least privilege with one BLISS and one BRIM role.
  2. Why is an unlinked file URL not sufficient protection for a restricted archival scan?
  3. Name the six NIST CSF 2.0 functions and briefly describe each.
  4. Explain how an institution should respond to suspected ransomware in a repository.
  5. What is a preservation event and what details should it capture?
  6. Explain the purpose of the NDSA Levels v2.1 framework and mention its sustainability emphasis.
  7. Differentiate business continuity planning from IT disaster recovery.

Formats and decisions

  1. Compare format migration with emulation for an interactive historical exhibition.
  2. Give four significant properties that could be validated after image or audio migration.
  3. Explain how sustainability considerations affect preservation planning without overriding legal duties.
  4. Develop a five-row risk register for a synthetic records archive.
  5. Describe the effects of a lost encryption key on confidential long-term records.

Applications

  1. Write a BLISS preservation plan for ten fictional digitised photographs.
  2. Write a BRIM recovery and restricted-access plan for a fictional committee records series.
  3. Identify three kinds of recovery evidence that would convince an auditor that a restoration worked.
